============================================================
1. PROJECT VISION
============================================================

CANONIX is an enterprise-grade AI-powered platform designed to
standardize, harmonize, classify and map material master data
across Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs), especially
organizations operating in Oil & Gas, Power, Steel, Mining and
Heavy Engineering.

Different CPSEs often maintain their own ERP/SAP material masters.

The same or functionally equivalent physical material can exist
under completely different:

- Material Codes
- Material Descriptions
- Abbreviations
- Units of Measurement
- Technical specifications
- Standards
- Grades
- Sizes
- Pressure ratings
- Classification structures
- Manufacturer references

Because of this, organizations cannot easily identify that two
material records represent the same physical or functionally
equivalent material.

CANONIX solves this problem by creating a common "Material Identity"
layer.

The system takes raw material data from individual CPSEs, preserves
the original data, cleans and normalizes descriptions, extracts
technical attributes, creates a structured Material DNA, generates
semantic embeddings, performs hybrid AI matching, explains the
matching decision, sends uncertain matches to human experts, and
finally creates a National Material Identity / National Material
Code while preserving every legacy CPSE code.

The central philosophy of CANONIX is:

DETECT
    ↓
UNDERSTAND
    ↓
NORMALIZE
    ↓
HARMONIZE
    ↓
VALIDATE
    ↓
GOVERN
    ↓
PREVENT
    ↓
OPTIMIZE

2. PROBLEM STATEMENT
============================================================

CPSEs across sectors such as Oil & Gas, Power, Steel, Mining and
Heavy Engineering use large material master databases in their
ERP/SAP systems.

Many of these organizations procure and use the same or similar
materials.

However, each organization may independently create material
records.

For example:

CPCL:
M-PIPE-00772

Description:
PIPE 8 BE, SEAMLESS, A106 Gr.B, STD
PIPE 8 INCH BE, SEAMLESS, ASTM A106 Gr.B,
SCH.STD, B-36.10

IOCL:
M-PIPE-00923

Description:
SMLS PIPE / 8"/ STD / A 106 Gr. B
SEAMLESS PIPE /8.00/ B36.10/STD/ASTM A106 Grade B

Another CPSE could have:

M-PIPE-01221

Description:
8 IN CARBON STEEL SMLS PIPE ASTM A106 GR B
SCH STD ASME B36.10

These descriptions look different as strings, but they can represent
the same material identity.

Similarly:

CPCL:
M-VLVE-00111
GATE VL. 8.00/RF/150/SHT51301

Another CPSE:
M-VLVE-00881
8 IN GATE VALVE RF CLASS 150 API 600

Another:
M-VLVE-01921
GATE VALVE 8" / RF / 150# / API600

A simple text comparison is not sufficient to determine whether
these materials are identical, near duplicates, functionally
equivalent or actually different.

The system therefore needs to understand:

- Engineering terminology
- Abbreviations
- Units
- Technical attributes
- Standards
- Material grades
- Size
- Pressure rating
- Connection types
- Application context
- Material categories
- Semantic meaning
- Historical procurement behavior

The objective is to create a trusted national material identity
framework that allows different CPSE material codes to map to one
common national material identity where appropriate.


============================================================
3. CORE OBJECTIVES
============================================================

CANONIX must achieve the following:

1. Accept raw material master data from individual CPSEs.

2. Ensure each CPSE can upload only its own data.

3. Preserve original CPSE material codes and descriptions.

4. Validate uploaded files.

5. Detect missing and invalid data.

6. Normalize inconsistent terminology and units.

7. Extract technical attributes automatically from descriptions.

8. Build structured Material DNA.

9. Generate semantic embeddings for material descriptions.

10. Find candidate materials across CPSEs.

11. Perform hybrid material matching.

12. Distinguish between:
    - SAME
    - NEAR DUPLICATE
    - FUNCTIONALLY EQUIVALENT
    - APPROVED SUBSTITUTE
    - RELATED
    - DIFFERENT

13. Provide explainable AI decisions.

14. Allow domain experts to approve/reject/modify AI results.

15. Learn from expert decisions.

16. Generate standardized canonical material descriptions.

17. Create National Material Identities.

18. Map all legacy CPSE material codes to National Material Codes.

19. Preserve legacy codes for migration and traceability.

20. Provide cross-CPSE procurement intelligence.

21. Identify aggregated national demand.

22. Identify duplicate procurement opportunities.

23. Prevent duplicate material creation in the future.

24. Provide dashboards, analytics, audit logs and governance.

4. IMPORTANT DATA OWNERSHIP MODEL
============================================================

Each CPSE uploads its own material master.

A CPCL user does NOT upload IOCL/BPCL/HPCL data.

Example:

CPCL LOGIN
    ↓
CPCL ADMIN
    ↓
UPLOAD CPCL FILE
    ↓
Backend automatically associates records with CPCL

IOCL LOGIN
    ↓
IOCL ADMIN
    ↓
UPLOAD IOCL FILE
    ↓
Backend automatically associates records with IOCL

The CPSE must NOT manually select its organization during upload.

The backend determines the CPSE from the authenticated user's
cpse_id.

This prevents accidental or malicious cross-organization data
assignment.

The central CANONIX AI engine can compare material identities across
CPSEs where permitted.

CPSE users should not be able to modify another CPSE's original
material data.

28. ENGINEERING RULES
============================================================

Engineering rules must override misleading text similarity.

Example:

Material A:

GATE VALVE
8 INCH
CLASS 300


Material B:

GATE VALVE
8 INCH
CLASS 150


Semantic similarity may be extremely high.

But pressure class differs.

Therefore:

NOT SAME

Potentially:

NEAR_DUPLICATE
or
RELATED

depending on the technical rules.


Another example:

Pipe:

A106 Gr.B

versus

A312 TP304

These should NOT be treated as identical merely because:

8 INCH PIPE

appears in both.


============================================================
29. MATCH RELATIONSHIP TYPES
============================================================

CANONIX should classify relationships as:

1. SAME

Same material identity.

2. NEAR_DUPLICATE

Very similar records but with minor differences/noise.

3. FUNCTIONALLY_EQUIVALENT

Different specifications/codes but technically serve the same
function under defined rules.

4. APPROVED_SUBSTITUTE

Can be substituted according to approved technical rules.

5. RELATED

Same material family but different technical identity.

6. DIFFERENT

Technically different materials.


============================================================
30. MATCH SCORE THRESHOLDS
============================================================

Example configuration:

90-100:
HIGH CONFIDENCE

75-89:
MEDIUM CONFIDENCE

Below 75:
LOW CONFIDENCE


But final relationship should depend on both score and technical
rules.

Example:

Score = 96%

Pressure mismatch

Final:
NOT SAME

Therefore, numeric similarity alone must never control the final
decision.
